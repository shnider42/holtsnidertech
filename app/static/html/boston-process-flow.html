Solutions Engineering + Opportunity Engineering

The starting point is not always a ticket. Sometimes something is broken. Sometimes something works, but the product, process, customer experience, or conversion path could create more value.

Step 1 / Choose the lane

What are you trying to change?

Pick the closest starting point. The next section narrows it into a useful first conversation.

Recommended lane / Solutions Engineering

Fix the break. Clarify the system. Stabilize the path forward.

This is the lane for a concrete problem: a failing technical system, unclear vendor explanation, broken workflow, risky rollout, unreliable process, or team that needs another technical lens.

Good first conversation
  • What changed?
  • Who is affected?
  • Where does the failure show up?
  • What evidence already exists?
Likely outputs
  • Root-cause notes
  • Tradeoff map
  • Fix or workaround
  • Implementation or support path

Recommended lane / Opportunity Engineering

Find the leverage point. Shape the better path. Prototype what could help.

This is the lane for less obvious value: improving customer experience, lead conversion, retention, product clarity, internal workflow, content systems, or a promising idea that needs shape.

Good first conversation
  • What outcome should improve?
  • Where do people get stuck?
  • What behavior should change?
  • What would make the product or process more useful?
Likely outputs
  • Opportunity map
  • Process or customer-flow review
  • Feature direction
  • Small prototype or demo

Recommended lane / Discovery

The useful first step is naming the shape of the work.

This is for the messy middle: you know something needs attention, but you are not sure whether it is a technical fix, a workflow problem, a product opportunity, a customer-experience gap, or all of the above.

Useful prompts
  • What feels expensive, confusing, or repetitive?
  • Who feels the pain first?
  • What decision are you trying to make?
  • What would be better if this worked?
Likely outputs
  • Clearer problem frame
  • Solutions vs. Opportunity recommendation
  • Next-step map
  • Decision on whether to fix, shape, prototype, or pause

What is happening, being requested, or getting in the way?
